How We Built Seolith Tiles From the Ground Up (Rust + WASM + Three.js + Cloudflare Edge) — And How It Monetizes

Published by Seolith LLC Engineering • October 18, 2026 • 10 min read • seolith-tiles.seolith-com.workers.dev

If you have scrolled through X (Twitter), TikTok, or Reels lately, you have almost certainly paused to watch those mesmeric micro-tile brick breaker videos (like @iwhale's viral Diagonal Chimney and Center Bent Pipe posts). A single ball threads a razor-thin steel shaft, ricochets off a 45° corner into a cluster of gold multiplier blocks, and suddenly 1,500+ balls erupt inside a sealed chamber of 4,096+ tiny bricks accompanied by rapid-fire ASMR woodblock pops.

Most of those viral clips are offline physics renders—you can't actually play them in your browser, customize the screen, or compete on global/local leaderboards. We set out to build Seolith Tiles: a lightweight, 60–120 FPS interactive web and mobile game built from scratch using Rust (compiled to WebAssembly), Three.js (InstancedMesh), Procedural WebAudio, and Cloudflare Workers + D1 SQLite.

1. The Engineering Challenge: Why Naive JavaScript Breaks at 1,600 Balls

Simulating 1,600 simultaneous high-speed balls colliding against 6,400 micro-tiles at 60–120 frames per second requires evaluating millions of potential interactions per second:

  • The Tunneling ("Bleeding") Problem: At high velocities, a ball moves further in a single 16.6ms frame than the thickness of a 1-tile steel wall. Without continuous sub-stepping and closest-point Circle-vs-AABB resolution, balls "bleed" right through diagonal steel chutes.
  • The Draw-Call Bottleneck: Creating 6,400 individual Three.js Mesh objects and 1,600 ball meshes causes 8,000+ WebGL draw calls per frame, dropping mobile browsers to 5 FPS.
  • Garbage Collection Stutters: Allocating collision vectors or audio nodes in JavaScript every frame triggers GC pauses that ruin the tactile ASMR rhythm.

Pillar 1: Zero-Copy Rust WebAssembly Physics (src-rust/lib.rs)

We wrote the physics engine in pure #![no_std]-style Rust with zero heap allocations during gameplay. Every entity lives in a fixed, cache-aligned static mut STATE: EngineState struct compiled to a tiny 22 KB seolith_engine.wasm binary.

  • O(1) Spatial Grid Lookup: Instead of checking all 6,400 bricks per ball, we convert the ball's (x, y) coordinate directly into grid indices (col, row) and inspect only the 3×3 neighborhood around the ball.
  • 16x Sub-Stepping & Multi-Pass Circle-vs-AABB Solver: Each frame is subdivided into up to 16 micro-steps so a ball never travels more than 22% of a tile width per sub-step. Within each sub-step, we prioritize indestructible TILE_WALL_STEEL blocks first and push the ball out along the exact minimum-penetration vector.
  • Watertight 2-Tile-Thick Diagonal Chutes: All 45° chimneys are reinforced with 4-connected double steel walls so diagonal corner contacts are 100% airtight.

Pillar 2: 4-Draw-Call Three.js InstancedMesh Renderer (src/renderer.js)

JavaScript never copies arrays back and forth from Rust. Instead, Three.js reads Rust's linear WebAssembly memory directly via typed array views (new Uint8Array(wasmMemory.buffer, get_tiles_ptr(), 6400)) and updates just 4 GPU InstancedMesh buffers:

  1. brickMesh (up to 6,400 micro-tiles with custom colors & 3D depth)
  2. ballMesh (up to 1,600 active cascade balls)
  3. capsuleMesh (up to 64 falling power-up pills: x3, +3, <->, [-], ★)
  4. particleMesh (up to 1,200 3D shatter particles)

Pillar 3: Zero-Asset Procedural WebAudio ASMR Synthesizer (src/audio.js)

Why does the viral video feel so satisfying? Because the pitch rises as balls climb deeper into the upper vault! Whenever Rust records brick hits in a frame, it exposes get_last_hit_row_norm() (from 0.0 at the bottom to 1.0 at the top). Our WebAudio synthesizer maps that row height to a pentatonic marimba/woodblock frequency with micro-jitter and a 118 BPM synthwave bassline—requiring 0 KB of external MP3 files.

Pillar 4: Cloudflare Workers + D1 Edge Backend (worker/index.js)

Every request hits a Cloudflare Worker backed by a serverless SQLite database (Cloudflare D1) and automatic request.cf geo-IP metadata:

  • Time & Location Leaderboards (/api/leaderboard): Ranks players by fastest clear time and highest score across Daily, Weekly, Monthly, and Yearly windows filtered by Global, Country, or Region/City.
  • Protected Admin Geo-Analytics (/api/admin/analytics): Tracks visitor countries, regions, cities, Cloudflare Edge colos, device split (Mobile vs. Desktop), and viral ?ref= referral tags for authorized admin accounts.
  • Live Tester Feedback Loop (/api/feedback): Lets players submit bugs and ideas that sync directly into our repo via npm run feedback:read.

3. How Seolith Tiles Is Monetized (Day-1 + 100-Play Hybrid Funnel)

Casual web games fail to monetize when they slap intrusive banner ads before the player is hooked—or when they wait too long and never offer an upgrade path to high-intent players. We implemented a 4-tier hybrid monetization architecture:

Monetization Tier Trigger Point Price / Value Exchange Technical Implementation
1. Viral Share-to-Boost Loop Day 1 (Every Run) FREE (1-Click X / WhatsApp / Challenge Link share = +3 ⚡ x3 Boosts) Generates ?ref=Handle&level=0&target=42.8 challenge URLs that bring new players in at $0 CAC.
2. Interactive 5-Second Sponsor Spot When out of ⚡ x3 Boosts or at the 100-play gate FREE to Player (5s partner spotlight = +3 Boosts & +5 Plays) Served via GET /api/monetization with a 5-second countdown timer, clickable sponsor CTA, and D1 telemetry (sponsor_watch).
3. Impulse Micro-Pack (+15x Boosts) Pro Shop Modal (👑 Pro button) $0.99 USD Processed via POST /api/checkout (`boost_pack_15`) and logged to the D1 monetization_orders table.
4. Lifetime Seolith Pro Pass (100-Play Gate) Anytime via 👑 Pro or after 100 Free Plays $2.99 USD Lifetime Unlocks unlimited plays past 100, automatic Gold Safety Floor at launch, ∞ x3 Boosts, and Verified Pro badge.
💡 Why the 100-Play Gate + Day-1 Pro Shop Works:
Players who just want a quick 2-minute dopamine break can buy a $0.99 Boost Pack, watch a 5-second Sponsor Spot, or share on X on their very first session. Power players who hit 100 plays have proven deep retention and convert at high rates to the $2.99 Lifetime Pro Pass.
